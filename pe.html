The business

Two sides, one room. Events are where they meet.

Engineers come for knowledge, peers and their careers. Sponsors pay to meet the people who choose and buy their tools. I've sat on the sponsor's side, choosing events, side events and VIP dinners, so I know what makes a sponsor renew: qualified attendance, not registrations.

Stage 1

Awareness & acquisition

Start from the data we already have, then add partners and new channels.

  • HubSpot research and segmentation. Clean up and enrich the database: events attended, attended vs no-show, city, role, company.
  • ICP score and tiers. Fit, engagement and intent give a score from 0 to 100 and tiers A, B and C.
  • Sync with performance and lifecycle. One brief per event, with goals set in qualified attendees. Lookalikes from tier A, LinkedIn targeting by title and target accounts, email sequences per tier.
  • City partners. Meetups, communities, newsletters and local leaders, tiered by audience fit, with tracked codes.
  • New channels as tests. Web3 infrastructure teams, AI engineering communities, DevRel networks and interactive lead magnets.
Me: ownerPerformanceLifecyclePartners

Measure: new contacts per tier · ICP share by source

Stage 2

Qualify & convert

Every registration tells us who is coming.

  • Registration questions on Luma. Role, company, team size and whether they're evaluating tools in the next 12 months.
  • Approval rules by tier. A: instant approval and a Leaders Track invite. B: approved. C: waitlist or the virtual option when capacity is tight.
  • A message per segment. What an engineer gets is not what a CTO gets.
Me: ownerContentDesignOps

Measure: registration rate · share of tiers A and B

Stage 3

Activate & show up

Registration is a promise. Attendance is the result.

  • Pre-event workflow. Confirmation, warm-up content, who's in the room, then reconfirm or release your spot 48h before.
  • Attendance tests. Speaker invites, team tickets, a convince-your-manager kit and perks that only exist in person.
  • Pre-booked sponsor meetings for tier A (opt-in). Value for both sides before doors open.
Me: ownerLifecycleSponsorship

Measure: show-up rate by tier · decision makers present

Stage 4

Connect on site

Make the room work for both sides.

  • Check-in synced to HubSpot. So we know who actually attended.
  • Leaders Track. Peer roundtables, a lounge and a VIP dinner. CTOs come for their peers.
  • Capture interests. Sessions and booths visited, used to personalise the follow-up.
Events teamMe: data & VIPSponsors

Measure: sponsor meetings held · session engagement

Stage 5

Prove value

The step most event businesses skip.

  • For attendees. Recordings, resources and a clear next step. One follow-up for attendees, another for no-shows.
  • For sponsors. An audience report (tier mix, companies, meetings), followed by the pipeline they report back.
  • NPS on both sides.
Me: ownerSponsorshipLifecycle

Measure: attendee NPS · sponsor satisfaction

Stage 6

Retain & advocate

Attendees and sponsors become a channel.

  • Attendees. They come back, refer colleagues, become speakers or city ambassadors, and get certified.
  • Sponsors. They renew, upgrade to VIP dinners or custom events, and promote the event to their own customers.
Me: ownerCommunitySponsorship

Measure: repeat attendance · referral share · sponsor renewal

Stage 7

Learn & re-segment

The data loop that makes the wheel faster.

  • Update scores with real attendance. No-shows lose points. People who come back gain them.
  • Rank partners, channels and tests. By cost per qualified attendee who actually showed up.
  • New lookalikes and a sharper ICP. These feed straight back into stage 1.

Measure: cost per qualified attendee over time

ICP score

Know who's in the room before they arrive.

A simple scoring model in HubSpot, updated after every event. Weights are a starting point and would be tuned with real data.

Score (0–100)

Fit
50Role and seniority, company size, engineering team size, cloud-native stack
Engagement
30Past events, Slack, newsletter, courses and certifications
Intent
20Evaluating tools in the next 12 months, on a sponsor's target account list
A

Decision makers. CTOs, VPs Engineering, Heads of Platform and AI infra. 1:1 invites, Leaders Track, VIP dinners.

B

Technical influencers. Staff and principal engineers, platform leads, architects. Segmented campaigns and speaker invites.

C

Practitioners. The heart of the community. Content, meetups, the virtual flagship and a path towards A and B.
