04Growth & retention flywheelEvery event increases the value and the reach of the next one+

Six stages around one goal: community and audience growth, city by city. Select a stage to see what happens in it, who owns it and what we measure.

Community
& audiencegrowth
Stage 1

Attract

Build each city's audience from the data we already have, then add partners and new channels.

  • HubSpot research and segmentation. Events attended, attended vs no-show, city, role and company.
  • Segments that matter. Role, seniority, city, company and past attendance.
  • Sync with performance and lifecycle. Goals set in qualified attendees. Lookalikes from past leaders, LinkedIn targeting by title, email per segment.
  • City partners and leaders. Meetups, communities, newsletters and local leaders, with tracked codes.
  • New markets as tests. AI engineering communities for AgenticCon, and Web3 infrastructure teams (Layer 5).
Me: ownerPerformanceLifecyclePartners

Measure: city audience size · decision makers by source

Stage 2

Register

A Luma page that converts, and registrations that tell us who's coming.

  • Event page with design and content. Strong visuals and copy for every event.
  • Registration questions. Role, company, team size and whether they're evaluating tools.
  • Approval rules. Leaders approved instantly and invited to the leaders track; a waitlist when space is tight.
  • A message per segment. What an engineer gets is not what a CTO gets.
Me: ownerDesignContent

Measure: page conversion · qualified share

Stage 3

Attend

Registration is a promise. Attendance is the result.

  • Attendance engine. Who's-in-the-room emails, reconfirm or release 48h before, calendar holds and a day-of message.
  • Attendance tests. Speaker invites, team tickets, a convince-your-manager kit and perks that only exist in person.
  • Check-in synced to HubSpot. Reliable attendance data for every person.
Me: ownerLifecycleEvents team

Measure: attendance rate by segment

Stage 4

Engage

Keep the audience warm between events, the way we kept Golden Sats participants active across four seasons.

  • Follow-ups. Recordings and resources for attendees; a second chance for no-shows.
  • City rhythm. City Luma calendars, local meetups, newsletter blocks and community content.
  • Audience profile to sponsorship. Who came and what they cared about, so sponsors meet the right people.
Me: ownerLifecycleCommunitySponsorship

Measure: engaged share of each city's audience

Stage 5

Advocate

Turn your best attendees into the voice of the next event.

  • Ambassadors as city hosts. Ambassador Program members and meetup organisers co-host warm-up meetups and get their own codes.
  • PlatformCon Alumni. Early access, an alumni price and a returning-attendee badge.
  • Speakers and certified engineers. A speaker kit with "I'm speaking" cards, plus an in-person moment for certified engineers.
Me: ownerCommunityContent

Measure: repeat attendance · active advocates per city

Stage 6

Refer

Every attendee brings the next one, and the data makes the wheel faster.

  • Referrals and team invites. "Bring your platform lead" right after registering, plus "I'm attending" cards.
  • Partners back. The best-performing partners return for the next event.
  • Learn and re-segment. Update scores with real attendance and rank channels by cost per attendee. This feeds straight back into Attract.
Me: ownerPartnersOps

Measure: share of registrations from referrals, ambassadors and partners

The play

  1. Build the listCrypto and fintech companies sponsoring major events, with confirmed platform or AI teams, starting from my own contacts.
  2. Invite their leaders 1:1Heads of Platform, Infrastructure and AI to PlatformCon and AgenticCon, with the team offer for their engineers.
  3. One curated roundtable"Platform engineering for Web3 infrastructure", inside an existing KubeCon satellite, or as a VIP dinner at Devcon 9 in Lisbon (11–13 Oct 2027).
  4. Hand accounts to salesEvery vendor account goes to the sponsorship team as a sponsor prospect.

Decision rule: scale only if 40%+ of registrants match the target profile, show-up is at or above average, and sales sees real sponsor interest. Otherwise stop. I couldn't find any Web3-focused content in the Platform Engineering ecosystem, so this is white space worth one small test.
